The Council Chamber hung like a glass lantern above the gas giant, its curved command deck awash in the bruised amber light of the planet's storms. Holographic ledgers shimmered over the obsidian table, columns of figures and sealed authorizations sliding through the air while the station's distant ventilation hummed like a held breath. Elena stood at the head of the table, calm in the way of deep water, her hands folded over a stack of confidential reports. Marcus, the veteran commander, occupied the opposite chair with the easy confidence of a man who had survived worse rooms, though his gaze kept drifting toward the sealed blast doors. Julian, the young treasurer, sat too straight, his fingers worrying the edge of a datapad, and at the chamber's margin lingered the hooded courier, watchful and silent beneath a travel-stained cloak.

Elena lifted the top dossier, its red seal catching the light, and turned toward Marcus. Her voice carried no heat, only the precision of a scientist measuring a volatile sample. "Molim vas da pažljivo pregledate ove poverljive izveštaje pre nego što orbitalna straža zatvori kapije." The words settled into the room like a command disguised as courtesy. Marcus inclined his head, already reaching for the papers, but before his fingers could close on them, the hooded courier stepped forward and bowed low, the cloak pooling around her boots. The gesture was formal, almost theatrical, yet her voice emerged muffled and careful. "Ponizno vam donosim zapečaćene depeše sa severne granice koje zahtevaju vašu hitnu vojnu procenu."

Marcus took the depeches without haste, testing their weight, while Julian's nervousness sharpened into something almost audible. The young treasurer glanced from Elena to the courier and back again, as though trying to solve an equation whose variables kept changing. He murmured, barely more than breath, "Yes, indeed." The two words fell flat and awkward, swallowed by the chamber's vast acoustics. Elena did not look at him, but her calm seemed to tighten by a fraction, a wire drawn one notch closer to breaking.

The inspection continued beneath the slow wheel of the gas giant. Records were opened, seals compared, timestamps cross-checked against the orbital watch. Every click of a datapad seemed louder than it should have been. The hooded courier remained at the edge of the light, watchful, while Marcus read with the patience of a soldier who trusted documents only after he had smelled their ink. Julian's breathing had gone shallow. Outside the panoramic shield, lightning crawled through the planet's upper clouds, and the Council Chamber held its breath around them all.

Elena's fury did not break; it hardened. She had trusted him implicitly. When the northern delegations demanded concessions, Julian had found compromises in the margins; when the council wavered, he had whispered the right figures into the right ears. Elena had signed the pact with his encouragement ringing in her memory, convinced that the young treasurer was the one honest constant in a station full of shifting alliances.

The memory sharpened around the signing table. Seals had been pressed into wax; the Obsidian Signet had lain in its case like a sleeping eye. Julian had smiled at her across the documents, and she had smiled back, grateful for his cleverness. The winter wind had howled against the shielded balcony doors even then, though Marcus had not yet opened them. The council had applauded. The pact had seemed, for one bright and foolish moment, like the beginning of something stable.

Now, in the present, Elena stood alone in the Council Chamber with that memory burning behind her eyes. The fury was still there, preserved exactly as she had left it, untouched by the flashback. Ten years ago she had trusted Julian implicitly. Ten years ago she had been naive_trusting. The realization did not soften her; it sharpened the edge of her anger. The room returned around her, the same glass, the same table, the same amber light, but the woman standing in it was no longer the one who had signed that pact.

Down in the Archive Vault, frost breathed from the cryogenic racks and the blast doors sealed the world above into a distant rumor. Julian's shock was almost comic: his mouth fell open, and the name escaped him in a broken whisper. "Lyra?" "Predaj mi sve lažne povelje odmah, Julijane, ili ćeš do zore biti u kraljevskoj tamnici!" Lyra's voice rang through the vault, no longer muffled, no longer humble. Julian flinched as if struck. His hands rose, empty and trembling, then fell again. The Obsidian Signet glinted in Lyra's gloved fist. Somewhere above them, the station's night cycle had begun, and the vault's frost seemed to settle deeper into the metal.

Moments later, Marcus stepped back into the Council Chamber from the Windy Balcony, brushing wind-driven cold from his sleeves. The heavy doors had barely closed when Julian came up the rear stairs from the vault, pale and breathless, his coat torn at one seam. Marcus stopped. Elena turned from the table, her fury banked but still burning beneath the surface. Julian straightened his collar and spoke before anyone could ask a question. Northern raiders, he said, had broken into the annex. They had stolen the Obsidian Signet while he defended the ledger. He had barely escaped, he claimed, and the vault was in chaos.

The lie landed in the chamber and spread like oil on water. Marcus's expression darkened with concern, not suspicion. Elena watched Julian's performance with her jaw set. Though still furious inside, she let her voice go light and dry, each word a small blade wrapped in silk. "Oh, what a wonderfully convenient tale of heroic vigilance, dear cousin." Julian did not meet her eyes. Lyra remained down in the Archive Vault, holding the signet and the truth, while the Council Chamber above filled with the fragile silence of a story no one believed.

All four of them were together now beneath the gas giant's amber gaze: Elena, Marcus, Julian, and Lyra.

"Kako se usuđuješ da obmanjuješ vojni savet dok držimo dokaze u rukama!" The bark of his voice rebounded from the curved ceiling. Julian shrank back against the table, cornered, his eyes darting from Marcus to Elena to Lyra and finding no shelter in any of them.

Elena said nothing. She did not need to. The fury she had preserved through the long dusk was now fully visible, and it was colder than the vault below. Julian's hands fumbled at his coat, then found the small vial he had hidden in an inner seam, a memory-erasing draught prepared for exactly such an emergency. He uncorked it and swallowed before anyone could stop him. The damper worked quickly, a dim pulse behind his eyes, a slackening of the face.

Julian blinked, swayed, and looked around the Council Chamber as though waking from a dream. The ten-years-ago flashback event, the winter crisis, the pact signing, the trust Elena had once placed in him, all of it vanished from his memory in a single dissolving stroke. He stared at the Obsidian Signet on the table and did not know why his hands were shaking. Marcus drew a sharp breath; Lyra's determined expression turned grim; Elena watched the young treasurer become a stranger to his own guilt. Above them, the gas giant rolled on, indifferent, while the Council Chamber filled with the terrible quiet of a truth that could no longer be remembered.
